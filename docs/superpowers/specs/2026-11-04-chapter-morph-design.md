# The chapter morph: moving between chapters of the story

Written on 4 Nov 2026 from the owner's note. It replaces the logo curtain for moves between two chapters of the book. Everything else on the site (menu, landing, contents sheet, every other page) keeps the logo transition.

## The owner's note (word for word)

> See the /story page. I wanted you to remake the transitions between chapter when user is reading.
>
> So on bottom of the chapter page, there's a button to go to the next chapter. I wanted you to remake the transition completely and do NOT use the default transitions.
>
> So first, on the next page preview itself on bottom of the chapter page, I wanted it to be animated, so when it's video, make sure the video is playing. Then when user clicks the next chapter, I wanted the button to MORPH and adjust it's sizing, position and so much more to the next chapter hero. So for example the visuals like the image or video will be zoomed in to full and cover the page, and the text will position itself. I wanted the animations to be smooth and beautiful. You will think of everything to put in this animation and make sure it's performant, cool, and creative. ## What exists now

- `features/story/reader/ChapterEnd.tsx`: at the foot of a chapter, a card for the next chapter (the still `thumb`, or the sealed twin for a chapter whose picture gives something away), a link "Before: ...", and "All chapters". The links are `TLink`s, so they go through the logo curtain (`components/transition/transition-provider.tsx`: the logo zooms out, the route changes, the logo zooms in, with a loading state after 500 ms).
- `features/story/reader/TitleCard.tsx` + `HeroMedia.tsx`: the chapter's opening, a full-screen (`100svh`) picture (still or muted loop) under `.cardShade`, with `.cardText` (act and chapter line, title, dateline, mood) bottom left and a cue. `.cardMedia` has a 30 s slow push (`card-push`), and the picture and text drift away on scroll (CSS scroll-driven animations).
- The reader keeps the top bar locked (`lib/nav-lock.ts`: `theme`, `bare`), a mini bar (`MiniBar.tsx`), a reading engine (`engine.ts`), the chapter music (`score.ts`), the page loading list (`lib/route-preloads.ts`, `reader/preload-items.ts`: a chapter's title card picture or loop is held whole in memory by `lib/media-store.ts`), Lenis smooth scrolling (`components/smooth-scroll.tsx`, `useLenis()` with `pause`, `resume`, `scrollTo`, and `getLenis()` whose `on("virtual-scroll")` reports wheel deltas even at the end of the page).
- `index.json` entries (`ChapterEntry`) carry `first[0]` (the title card's file: `kind`, `src`, `poster`, `bytes`), `thumb`, `sealed`, `color`, `act`, `dateline`, `mood`, `title`, `number`. The focus point of the title card (`heroFocus`, an `object-position`) is only in the chapter file: add `focus` to the entry in `scripts/story/build.py` (and `ChapterEntry`) and rebuild.

## The rules that bind this work (from `CLAUDE.md`)

Animate `transform` and `opacity` only (no width, height, top, left, margin, `filter`, `backdrop-filter`, `mix-blend-mode`, and no per-frame writes to `<html>` or `<body>`). Prefer the Web Animations API (compositor) to a frame loop. One version only: no device gating, and the system's reduced-motion setting is ignored on this site (there is a test for that). Never fake progress, never use a timer that pretends to load; a slow connection waits (the next chapter's first-paint files are in `routeItems(path)`). Use `chapterHref` for chapter links. The text of a chapter stays in the page's HTML; the morph is an enhancement on top of real `<a href>` links (modified clicks, middle click and no-JS keep working). No em dashes or en dashes anywhere in text or comments, US spelling.

## The design

### 1. The doorstep card (the next-chapter preview, animated)

The card at the foot of a chapter becomes a **miniature of the next chapter's title card**, not a separate thumbnail. It holds a "stage": a box the size of the viewport (the title card is `100vw` by `100svh`) containing exactly what the next title card holds (the picture with `object-fit: cover` and the chapter's `focus`, the shade), scaled down uniformly to fill the card (scale `s0 = max(cardW / vw, cardH / vh)`, set from a `ResizeObserver`, never per frame) and clipped by the card; the stage is shifted vertically so the focus point sits in the middle of the visible band, clamped to the stage. So the card shows a widescreen band of the real hero, with the same crop, and a morph can open that band without any distortion or crop jump.

- When the next chapter's title card is a clip (`next.first[0].kind === "clip"` and the entry is not sealed) the card's picture is a muted looping `<video>` (source through `mediaUrl`, poster from `first[0].poster`) that plays while the card is on screen (use `useAutoplay`) and pauses off screen. Sealed chapters and still heroes show the still (`sealed ?? thumb` for the sealed twin; the hero still for the rest).
- Warm the next chapter early: when the card is within about one screen of the viewport, run `runPreload(routeItems(nextPath))` and `router.prefetch(nextHref)` once, so the clip is in memory and the morph can start at once. If the visitor acts before the files have arrived, the card shows the real progress (a hairline along its foot, the percent in its kicker) and starts the morph by itself the moment the page is ready. No logo curtain.
- The text on the card keeps the current design (a small "Next" line, the chapter label, the big title, the dateline), as separate elements that the morph can move one by one: label, title, dateline, "Next" (fades out), plus the extra title-card lines that fade in (the act line, the mood, the scroll cue).
- The card is a real `<a href>` (a `TLink` with `transition={false}` and its own click handler). Hover and focus: the stage drifts and brightens a little (transform and opacity only).

### 2. The forward morph (next chapter), about 1.2 to 1.5 s

Triggered by a plain click or Enter on the card, or by the pull gesture (section 3). One overlay layer above the page and under the top bar, mounted once in the root layout (it must survive the route change), built from the next chapter's data; it is `aria-hidden` and `pointer-events: none` except for the viewer's focus rules (do not trap focus).

1. **Swap in.** The overlay shows exactly what the card shows, in the same place, pixel for pixel (its own `<video>` with `currentTime` copied from the card's and already playing; swap only after the overlay has painted a frame; never show a blank frame). The card's own picture then hides.
2. **Open the window** from the card's rectangle to the full viewport, with the content growing from `s0` to 1, by a transform-only clip: a wrapper `W` of viewport size, `transform-origin: 0 0`, `overflow: hidden`, animated `translate(x, y) scale(w / vw, h / vh)`; inside it the content layer `C` is counter-scaled so it is never stretched: `transform: scale(s / (w / vw), s / (h / vh))` about the wrapper's centre, with `s` the content's real uniform scale and the focus shift following the window. Both are driven by keyframes computed in JS at 20 to 30 samples with the easing baked in (so the inverse stays exact), played with the Web Animations API on the compositor. Unit-test the pure math (`morph-math.ts`: the keyframes, window rectangle and content scale at t=0 equal the card, at t=1 equal the viewport; the product of the two scales equals `s` at every sample).
3. **Move the text.** Each text element of the card travels to its place in the real title card with a transform (translate and uniform scale from the card rectangle to the final rectangle, measured once from a hidden copy laid out with the real `.card` CSS), staggered a little (the title leads, the label and dateline follow, 40 to 80 ms apart). The text is laid out at its final size and scaled down at the start, so it is sharp at the end. "Next" fades out in the first quarter; the act line, the mood and the cue rise 12 px and fade in during the last third; the title can reveal word by word if it looks better.
4. **The world behind lets go.** A scrim under the window fades from 0 to about 0.6 (opacity only); the page chrome that is on screen (mini bar, the card's neighbours: the "Before" row, "All chapters") fades out; the top bar goes bare (`useNavLock.setState({ bare: true })`) so it is already the bar of a title card when the window lands. A soft glow in the next chapter's colour (`--tint`, the entry's `color`) can bloom behind the window and fade (an element that scales and fades, not a box-shadow animation). Sounds: `audio.blip("whoosh")` at the start and `audio.blip("star")` when it lands, as the logo curtain does.
5. **Hand over.** When the window is full: `router.push(href, { scroll: false })` (prefetched already), wait for the path and `whenPageReady()`, then `scrollTo(0, { immediate: true })` and `window.scrollTo(0, 0)`, dispatch `REVEAL_EVENT`, focus the new `<main>` (as the provider's `focusMain` does), and remove the overlay on the next frame; the real title card is identical underneath. The new title card's video starts at the time the overlay's video had reached (a module-level hand-off read by `HeroMedia`, ignored if older than a few seconds), so the loop does not jump. Pause Lenis while it runs (`pause()` and `resume()`), ignore a second trigger while one runs, and give the old and new reading engines time to settle (the old engine saves the place on unmount).
6. Both directions have a **hard failure path**: if anything throws or the route does not change within a generous time while the files are fine, remove the overlay and navigate normally with `router.push(href)`. A visitor must never be left under a stuck overlay.

The last chapter's card leads to `/story/the-end`: the same morph, the card has no picture, so the window is the chapter's colour over the site's black with the text moving to the end page's title block (read `EndPage.tsx` for its header and match it).

### 3. The scroll threshold (the pull)

At the very end of the page (scroll position at the bottom, 1 px of tolerance) wheel, touch and keyboard input that tries to go further builds up a "pull" that is shown, then triggers the morph.

- A small indicator appears at the bottom centre (fixed, above the page, below nothing else), with the words "Keep scrolling" and the next chapter's label, a chevron that nudges, and a thin progress line that fills with the pull. The card answers the pull too (its stage scales up a few percent, its text lifts a little), all transform and opacity, driven by a CSS variable on the card element only (written at most once per animation frame, never on `<html>` or `<body>`).
- Wheel: take deltas from `useLenis().getLenis()?.on("virtual-scroll")` (Lenis intercepts the wheel), count only downward deltas while at the bottom, begin counting only after the page has been at rest at the bottom for about 250 ms or a new wheel gesture starts (a gap of 120 ms or more since the last event), so the tail of the momentum that carried the page down is not counted; the pull decays at about 600 px/s when no input arrives for 180 ms; the indicator shows from about 12 percent; the morph starts at about 650 px of pull. Touch: pull distance of the finger past the end (`touchstart` y minus current y) with a threshold near 140 px, no `preventDefault` (the listeners are passive). Keyboard: Space, PageDown, ArrowDown and End at the bottom add a step each (the card is also reachable and activated with Tab and Enter).
- After the morph starts, the pull is locked (cooldown) so momentum cannot start a second one.

### 4. Going back

**Scrolling up past the top (the pull up).** At the very top of a chapter that has a previous one, upward input builds the same kind of pull; the indicator sits at the top (below the top bar) and reads "Keep scrolling up" and the previous chapter's label, with the chevron pointing up. The chapter you are leaving is a full-screen title card here, so the morph can be the forward morph played in reverse: the overlay (an exact copy of the current title card, full viewport) shrinks and its text travels back into the **doorstep card of the previous chapter**, which is on screen at the foot of that chapter. Sequence: threshold reached, overlay appears (identical to the page), `router.push(previousHref, { scroll: false })` runs under it, the previous chapter opens scrolled to its end with its card placed in the lower middle of the viewport (centre it vertically), the overlay measures the card and shrinks into it with the same easing mirrored (the window closes, the content shrinks from 1 to `s0`, the text moves back, the scrim lifts), the overlay is removed; the real card is identical underneath. The mood is "closing the cover and stepping back onto the doorstep". (The saved place and the reading position update as usual.)

**The "Before: ..." link** at the foot of a chapter (the reader is at the bottom, no title card in view): a different, quicker move that suits "go back to the start of that chapter": the previous chapter's title card **unrolls from the top** over the current page like a blind being pulled down: the window grows from a thin strip at the top edge to the full viewport (clip transform as above, origin at the top), its content settling from a slight over-zoom (1.12) to 1 with the video playing, the title rising word by word from below, while the old page drops away a few percent (the scrim, and the chapter text under it dims). It lands on the top of the previous chapter exactly as the forward morph does (same hand-over). About 1.1 s. Give the link a small thumbnail or arrow so it reads as a button, and the same keyboard behaviour as the card.

The owner asked for creativity here: if the agent has a better idea that keeps the same hand-over and the same rules, it may use it, but it must explain it in the report and keep both ways back distinct from the forward morph.

### 5. Leave alone

The contents sheet, the landing's Continue, the menu and every other link keep the logo curtain. The mini bar's behaviour, the reading theme, the text size and the saved place keep working across the morph. The viewer (picture zoom) must not be open during a morph (close it first or ignore the trigger while it is open).

## As built (4 Nov 2026)

The code is in `apps/web/src/features/story/morph/`: `Door.tsx` (the card), `BeforeLink.tsx`, `ChapterPull.tsx` (both pulls and their indicators), `pull.ts` (the pull meter), `morph-math.ts` (the geometry), `morph-store.ts` (the running morph), `morph-layer.tsx` (in the root layout; the layer's code is a lazy chunk the card fetches ahead), `overlay.tsx` (the layer) and `choreo.ts` (the four runs: forward, end, back, blind). Where it differs from the design above, and why:

- **The window is two nested clips that only translate**, not a scaled wrapper with a counter-scaled content. The outer box clips the left and top edges, the inner box the right and bottom ones, a counter layer puts the content back in screen space, and the content has one uniform scale. Nothing is ever stretched, not even between keyframes, and every transform is linear in the eased progress, so each layer has two keyframes and one easing and the browser's own interpolation is exact (no sampled keyframes). `morph-math.test.ts` checks that the composed window and content equal the card at 0, the screen at 1, and the interpolation at every step in between.
- **The words move one by one.** The card's label, title and time line wear the title card's type at the card's size (the title is now italic, as the title card's is) and are split into word spans; the layer lays its copy out with the title card's own classes and moves each word from the card's word (its glyph box) to its own place with a translate and a uniform scale. Different line breaks in the card and the title card therefore never jump: the words reflow in flight. The title leads and the label and the time line follow 50 to 110 ms later; when the words travel up the screen (the end page) the label leads, so the lines never run into each other.
- **The stage of the card** is the title card's box scaled to fill the card on the focus point (`stageFit`), set by a ResizeObserver. Hover and the pull scale an inner layer about the middle of the visible band.
- **Lenis is not paused.** Its stopped state sets `overflow: clip` on the page, which hides the scrollbar and moves the page sideways under the card. The morph stops the smooth scroll where it is and holds the wheel, the touch and the scrolling keys with a capture listener, until the wheel has been quiet for 150 ms after the morph (the momentum of the pull runs out first).
- **The wheel comes from a passive window listener**, not from Lenis's `virtual-scroll`, so the pull does not depend on Lenis's life cycle; deltas are normalized by `deltaMode`.
- **The loops.** At the swap the layer draws the card's current frame on a canvas at once, so the first frame never waits; its own copy of the loop seeks to the same moment behind it and fades in when it plays. At the hand-over the new title card's loop is paused and moved to the layer's frame, and starts when the layer goes. This lives in `choreo.ts`; `HeroMedia` needs no hand-off. The new title card's slow push starts again at rest and its scroll cue keeps the layer's beat.
- **Focus** lands on the new chapter's heading (as `jumpTo` does), which the tests check; its focus ring shows only when the move was started from the keyboard (Enter on the card, a scrolling key at an end). The way back focuses `<main>`.
- **Never stuck, never lost.** Any error in a run removes the layer and opens the page with the curtain. A watchdog takes the layer away after 40 s whatever happens (a morph takes about two seconds, its files are in memory before it starts). A press on the card or on Before whose morph does not start (and none is running) opens the page the plain way.
- **The way back (the pull up)** sets the previous chapter at its end with its card in the middle of the screen, waits until the card holds still and the pictures on screen are decoded, then closes the window into it with the mirrored easing. "Next" comes back on the card in the last third; the bar gets its background back when the window leaves it.
- **The blind (Before)** has a lit edge in the chapter's color and a soft shadow that it throws on the page under it; the page drops 3 percent and darkens; the picture settles from 1.12 to 1 while the title rises word by word as the edge passes it. The link has a small picture of that chapter with an arrow.
- **The end page** lands on the reader's own background and ink (white in the light theme), not the site's black: the window opens in the chapter's color, turns into the page, and the words cross from white to the page's ink. The end page has more than its heading, so the rest of it comes in as the layer fades out. The last card's title is the end page's heading ("Who is who") and its second line "and when it all happened"; the heading's words are shared in `reader/end-copy.ts`. The end page's heading takes focus like a chapter's title.
- **Touch at the top:** a chapter page with a way back sets `overscroll-behavior-y: none` (`styles/base.css`), so pulling down there is the story's pull, not the browser's reload.
- **The probe hook** is `window.__chapterMorph`: set `freeze` (and `holdEnd`) before a press, wait for `phase` "held", `seek(ms)` for each frame, `release()`; with `holdEnd` it holds again at "landing", over the new page, until a second `release()`.
- **Fixed on the way:** after a move from one chapter to the next, the top bar lost its reading-theme lock (the old page cleared it after the new page had set it). The clear in `ReaderRoot` is now a layout cleanup, which runs first.
