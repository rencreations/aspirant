## The stream (`stream.ts`)

One module level `TrailerStream` that owns the one `<video>` (not three.js, so the loading list imports it). `crossOrigin = "anonymous"` is set before the first byte (without it the picture cannot be uploaded to WebGL: the bucket allows `aspirant.cc` and `localhost:3000/3100`, checked). The loading item (`stream:home-trailer`, `fx/video-plane/preload.ts`, weight 5, waited) calls `warmTrailer(report)`, which gives the element its file with `#t=11.7` and waits until 6 s are buffered from there. The film plane's plane 0 then takes the same element for its texture (`FilmVideo` with a `stream`), so the buffered seconds are the seconds that play. A visit whose list never ran still works: `FilmVideo.start()` starts the stream.

- **Playing.** muted, `playsinline`, no `loop` attribute: the stream goes back to `TRAILER_FILM.start` itself 0.12 s before the end (`toStart`) or on `ended`. It plays only while the plane is drawn on screen (`playPolicy` in `actor.ts`, as for every plane) and `pause()` holds it off screen. After the first seconds are in, `preload` is `metadata` until the plane plays (measured: the browser then asks for about 10 s of the film instead of 14 s or more while nobody watches), and `auto` again on `play()`.
- **A stall.** `waiting` pauses the element (it holds the last frame: the texture only takes new frames, so the old one stays on the GPU) and the stream plays again only when `RESUME_SECONDS` (3 s) are in hand (or the rest of the film, near its end), or, if a held element has stopped asking for more, when nothing new arrived for 1.5 s and at least 1.5 s are in hand (`PLATEAU_MS`, `PLATEAU_SECONDS`). That hysteresis is what keeps a link slower than the film (6.2 Mbit/s) from stuttering: it stops, fills 3 s, plays, instead of alternating every frame.
- **The loading line.** If the film has waited more than 1.2 s (`LINE_AFTER_MS`, a debounce, not fake progress) the hero plane draws a thin line along its foot (inside the rim, 2 px, the shader's `uLoad`): a faint track with the share of the 3 s in hand lit. It eases in and out at 5 a second. A short stall shows nothing, the held frame is the calm state. The line is also the first-load state if the first frame is late (the list never ran).
- **A stuck or failed download.** `waiting`, a loop or a seek that gets no new second or no new byte for `STALL_MS` (20 s, `lib/media-store.ts`) starts it again from where it stood: `recover()` unloads, keeps the time (a reload resets it to 0, so it is saved first and put in the address as `#t=`), and runs the same `attempt` as the first load under `untilItArrives` (`lib/retry.ts`: 1 s growing to 10 s, offline waits for the connection, never a timeout that gives up). `error` events do the same, after asking the server with a `Range: bytes=0-0` probe: 404 or 410, or a file this browser cannot play, ends as `failed` (the colour stays, nothing loops for ever); everything else is retried.
- **Lifetime.** `FilmVideo.dispose()` for the hero releases the stream (drops the file and the buffer, 145 MB stay out of memory); the home leaving (`NAVIGATED_EVENT`, any path that is not `/`) releases it too, for a visit where the plane never existed. The next visit makes a new one. The Films room's own pool is not touched: its element is keyed by film id and released by `keepFilms`, which is why the home does not share it.

## Not done, not verified

- **Not played on a phone, Firefox or Safari.** The cross origin video as a WebGL texture needs the bucket's CORS (checked on `localhost:3100`, the bucket also lists `aspirant.cc` and `localhost:3000`); on Safari a muted inline video in a texture should work but was not run. `preload` is a hint there and on a phone's Safari a film may load only once it plays: `attempt` already treats `suspend` with an idle network as ready.
- **The slow link was a route that serves 256 KB ranges, not a real throttled network.** Chrome's DevTools throttling does not slow the media element at all (checked: the buffer grew at full speed under 200 KB/s), so a real throttle was not possible. The route cuts every response to 256 KB and delays it, which is not the same as a slow TCP stream: in it a paused element stopped asking for more at about 2.4 s ahead for several seconds. That is why a hold resumes when nothing new has arrived for 1.5 s and at least 1.5 s are in hand (`PLATEAU_MS`, `PLATEAU_SECONDS`), not only at 3 s. On a real slow link the 3 s goal may be reached first; both end the hold.
- **Subtitles in the open window.** The film's own burned in English subtitles (the bottom 10 percent) and, from about 120 s, a song line at the very top, are in the picture. The strip's band hides them, the open window (the whole picture, as the owner asked) shows them. An owner call: cropping them would bring back the crop.
- **The orbit's leader is flatter** (248 by 104 at 1440 instead of 126 high) because it keeps the film's own shape. The ring is laid out for the old nominal card so nothing moved.
- **The previews were looked at on `previews-sheet.jpg`** (five frames across each clip, all ten read, no subtitle in any), not every frame: a subtitle that shows for under a second between those frames could be missed. Connection Lost was moved to 8.9 s after the first encode opened on a grey debris wipe.
- **The loader under a real throttle with the whole list** (all sections' deferred items) was not run; the order of the previews was checked by priority and by one dev run (all ten in the cache 0.8 s after the trailer's seconds), not against a throttled network, because the throttle does not reach the film and the previews are small local files in this setup.
- `layout.ts` (R-A3's edit) and `actor.ts` (a line of R-A3's `contentWindow` frame) are not mine; `prettier --check` still lists `layout.ts`. R-A3 owns the DOM window's shape in the strip (it should take `TRAILER_FILM.aspect`).
- `knip` lists 11 other unused exports of other helpers' files; none of mine.
- The stream's `events` and `retries` are probe data only.
