## What was found

1. **One static camera.** The background of `horizontal.mkv` never moves in 45 s, and the camera fitted on the idle and the one fitted on the pose agree to 0.6 degree. So the idle, the pose and both transitions use one camera. The game's swing between the idle camera and the pose camera is not in the recording.
2. **Its position is the game's** (0, 1.1053, 0.9545 in her space). With the position fixed to the game's and the field of view scanned, the landmark error has one sharp minimum at **60 degrees vertical** (rms 10.2 px of 1920 at 60, 11.5 at 61, 29.7 at 55, 25.7 at 65). The free fit (position unknown) gave the same position within 5 cm. The game's own data is a 51.28 degree lens and an unrolled camera: that was the "1.7 times tighter".
3. **Its rotation** is the game's with a roll: rotvec (-0.1065, -0.0230, -0.2586). The tilt of the recording (about 15 degrees) was never in the game's camera data.
4. **The sky is not an equirectangular sky.** Every equirectangular fit failed (the earlier attempt and three of mine: landmark pairs left 150 picture pixels of rms, SIFT RANSAC on the sphere collapsed to a degenerate lens). The recording shows the painting as a **flat picture bent by a smooth map**: a global affine map of the recording's frame onto the painting already matches the tower, the ring, the star and the right hand towers (rms 5.4 picture px on 180 SIFT inliers), and a cubic fits clearly better (cross validated median 3.0 picture px against 6.7 for the affine map on held out matches). That is why the star and the tower were 84 degrees apart on the sphere and 25 percent of the frame apart in the recording.

## How (so it can be repeated)

- Frames: ffmpeg at 1 fps (contact sheets) then full frames at 1920 by 864: idle at 0 s, pose at 20 s (idle again at 2.7, 36, 43 s). Timeline: idle 0 to 8 s, idle to pose about 8 to 10.5 s, pose 10.5 to 30.4 s, pose to idle about 30.4 to 32.5 s, idle after.
- 3D: `dumpmesh.py` (Blender 4.5, the entrance file) writes the skinned mesh and the bones, in her space (glTF), for the idle and the pose at 0 s and 1 s. The dragon is `abyssal_devil_02` of that file and is posed with her.
- Landmarks (read from zoomed crops with a grid): the two eyes (bones `Bone01_Eye_R_02` and `_L_02`) in both frames, the five claw tips of her right hand in the pose (the vertex furthest from the wrist in each finger group, matched by angle round the wrist), her right wrist in the idle. Ten points. `solve.py` and `fixfit.py` solve a pinhole camera (free, or with the position fixed) with the field of view scanned.
- Judging: a point splat of the posed mesh over the recording (`splat.py`, `show.py`) for both frames, then the real renderer against the recording (below). The first splat with the free fit already put the hair, the boots, the dragon and the hooks on the recording.
- Sky: `sift2.py` matches SIFT features of five recording frames to the painting (3348 matches), `aff.py` finds the best affine map by RANSAC, `warpfit.py` and `warpfit2.py` compare maps by held out error, `flowref2.py` refines the cubic three times with optical flow between the warped painting and the recording on the pixels where the character is absent (1262 samples), `mkpoly.py` writes `skyPoly`.

## What ships

- `fits.json` `landscape`: a Blender world matrix (row major), `vfov` 60, `shy` 0. `cameras_r7.py` writes it as one key for `city_idle`, `city_interaction_R_loop`, `city_interaction_R` and `city_interaction_back_R`. The portrait keys (`portrait`, CAM-portrait) are a crop of this camera.
- **The out clip** keeps the game's own camera path (`cam_city_intercation02`) with the idle's offset, so key 0 and the last key are exactly the fitted camera: the room's last rest camera equals the out clip's first camera (key 0 is (0, 1.1053, 0.9545), quaternion (-0.05305, -0.01147, -0.12886, 0.99018), 60 degrees). The camera swings back to about 5.6 m and returns. `outCameraFor` (the second branch of the out zone) reads the same data through `sl.cameraFor(OUT_CLIP, ..., { draw: false })`, so both branches of `scene.ts` use this camera and there is no handover jump of the camera; `out-camera.ts` was not changed. Looked at through `cameraFor` (not `frame`: the first sheet I made used `frame`, which holds the idle camera, and proved nothing) at 0, 0.4, 0.8, 1.5, 3, 4.4, 5, 5.6 and 6 s: `out2_sheet.png`. Her mesh in the out clip stays at least 0.133 m from the camera (at the rest pose; the near plane is 0.04 m): `outdist.py` over the real out clip mesh at 0.2 s steps.
- **The sky shader** (`sky.ts`): `uInv` (the frame's projection) gives the ray, `uTurn` (fromFilm times her root's inverse times the camera before the view turn) takes it to the fitted camera's frame, the tangents at 748.2 px of focal length give the recording's pixel (1920 by 864), the cubic gives the painting's pixel. `cameras-r7.json` `sky` is `{ fromFilm, poly }` (the old `fromFilm` equirectangular turn is gone). It is drawn in the same place with the same two draw mode of the cast. **Two guards for a camera that turns** (found in the out clip: a swing of about 31 degrees sent the rays outside the fitted frame, where the cubic blew up into streaks): the cubic is held at the edge of the fitted region (1.12 of the half frame) and goes on with its linear slope, the texture repeats mirrored, and the sky follows only `SKY_FOLLOW` = 0.35 of the camera's turn from the fitted camera (the painting is only about 0.4 of a frame wider than the recording's frame on each side). A still camera has no turn, so the idle, the pose, the view turn and the transitions are untouched. Said plainly: the sky does not follow the swing one to one, it is a very far sky.
- Parallax: the view turn is the existing `setView` (4 degrees of yaw and 2 of pitch about her anchor, the sky read before the turn). Computed on the final camera (`parmath.py`) for the four corners of a 1440 wide screen: the face moves up to 23 px, the hand 6 px, the feet up to 21 px (the other way), the pelvis 1 px, and the sky 0 px. Measured in the real renderer (template match): face 0 to 22 px, sky 0 px (the idle's own sway adds about 10 px). That is about 3 percent of the frame's height: slight, visible, and clearly 3D against a still sky.
