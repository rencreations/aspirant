# The rebuilt rooms of the collection (10 Nov 2026)

How the Video split on the shelf, the two Video rooms, Music and Animation work. The request and the calls made are in [`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-10-collection-redesign.md`](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-10-collection-redesign.md) and the owner's words in [`...-request.md`](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-10-collection-redesign-request.md). The first design of the library is in [`2026-11-04-collection-library-design.md`](../superpowers/specs/2026-11-04-collection-library-design.md).

Shared pieces: `features/library/shell/room-chrome.tsx` (`useTransparentNav`, `BackToCollection`), `features/library/video/links.ts` (`FILM_IDS`, `videoHref`, `videoRoute`), `features/library/data.ts` (`useLibraryData`, names `video-films` and `video-others`), `features/library/preloads.ts` (`libraryPageItems("video/films")`). The rebuilt rooms are full screen, have a top bar with no background and one button back to `/collection`. They do not link to each other and have no switcher.

## The Video split (shelf)

The Video cover is a gatefold: the front panel (Video outside, Films inside) turns on a hinge over the back panel (Others). One progress `p` moves at a constant speed toward 0 or 1 (open 980 ms, close 640 ms), and each part eases in its own window: the pair grows into place, the panel swings with a soft settle past flat, the halves part. The pair stays centred while it opens. Wide screens hinge on the left edge (Films left); phones flip up on the top edge (Films on top), or sit side by side on short phones. Shading and the panel's shadow are uniforms, so idle draws nothing. On phones the blurred stack is washed toward the ground while the cover is open. Under each half: its line, count and Open; Back sits under the pair (in the bar on phones). The open cover is in the address (`?open=video`, written with `replaceState`), so Back from a Video room returns to it. `/collection/video` is the plain page: two tiles with the same pictures as the halves. `window.__shelf` holds and seeks the split for frame sheets.

Files: `shelf/engine.ts`, `shelf/layout.ts` (`splitFrame`), `shelf/shelf-scene.tsx`, `shelf/video-halves.ts` (names, lines, colours), `shelf/cover-art.ts`, `shelf/preload.ts`, `video/chooser.tsx`, `video/chooser.module.css`. Covers `covers/video.webp`, `video-films.webp`, `video-others.webp` are made by `python scripts/library/covers.py video`. Per-room counts are in `index.json` under `rooms` (`build_index.py`). Spec: `e2e/library-shelf.spec.ts`.

**Room for the controls (small screens).** The focused cover and the open Video cover keep clear of the HTML controls over the scene. On a phone Search and Continue stand in a row under the tagline, and the phone's name bar sits at the foot. The scene reads their boxes once per layout (`shelf/obstacles.ts`, from the DOM, so a larger system font is followed) and `layout.ts` turns them into room (`focusRoom`, `focusPose`, `splitGeometry`). The focused cover moves down and shrinks only as far as it must to stand under Search and Continue and over the name bar, with its Open button above it. The stacked split starts under Continue. A tall phone and every wide screen keep the poses they always had, and a test pins six sizes.

A short screen is one under 540 px high (`SHORT_H`), which is a phone on its side. It has no room under the controls, so the cover and the pair stand between them, nudged sideways and shrunk when the controls leave less than they need. The index row fades out while a cover is focused or the split is open. The halves lose their italic line and keep a count and Open (a half under 9.5rem wide keeps only Open), and Back sits under the pair on a wide short screen or in the bar on a phone. `window.__shelf` also reports `focus`, `films` and `others`, the rects the scene draws, and the shelf spec compares them with Search, Continue, the name bar, the captions and Back at 390x844, 360x640, 375x667 and 844x390. File added: `shelf/obstacles.ts`.

## Video, Others (`/collection/video/others`)

One wall of every video that is not one of the ten Films: 92 videos in 13 shelves (`SHELVES` in `video-data.ts`; the pipeline's `ROWS` in `scripts/library/films/catalog.py` must match, a unit test checks it).

- Each tile has the shape of its picture. Rows are justified by CSS (`flex` grow by aspect ratio, one row height `--row-h`), and each shelf uses `content-visibility: auto` with a height estimated by `estimateShelf`.
- The finder is sticky under the bar: search, grouping (kind, pair or length), a filter sheet (character, pair, kind, canon, length, with live counts) and an index of the shelves that follows the scroll. A hover (mouse only) steps an 8 frame strip with a `transform` animation (`strip.webp`, made by `scripts/library/films/build.py`); no video is read until play.
- The viewer grows out of the tile with Web Animations, holds the scroll without pausing Lenis (`data-lenis-prevent`), and uses `VideoCard` with English captions, previous and next, a scenes list and more from the shelf.
- The address is `?i=<id>&t=<seconds>`. Opening pushes one history entry, so Back closes the viewer. The room remembers the video under the finder and the videos opened, in `aspirant:video-others`. A jump to a shelf builds every shelf, glides, then corrects until the page stops moving; any input ends the correction.
- Nothing starts by itself. Fan work and the parody use `CanonBadge`. Official edits and gameplay clips say "Not story".
- The top bar is the normal site bar (transparent at the top, solid once scrolled).
- Data: `video-others.json`, made by `scripts/library/films/build.py` (`catalog.py`, `reuse.py`, `chapters.py`) from a fresh scan of `D:\ren\aspirant\media`. It reuses the files already in the mirror (the old `films/<id>/` keys, the Design, Fanart and Others collection keys, and the site's own `public/video` copies); nothing is encoded twice. Left out: footage folders, the raw in-game animation recordings (they are the source of the Animation room), the 10 Films, a lower quality duplicate of the Revamp video, and one TikTok cosplay clip of a real person whose post the sources do not identify (it needs the owner's yes: one flag in `reuse.py`).
- Files: `features/library/video/others/` (`page.tsx`, `finder.tsx`, `wall.tsx`, `tile.tsx`, `viewer.tsx`, `video-data.ts`, `types.ts`, `preload.ts`, `use-open-video.ts`, `use-memory.ts`, `use-captions-blob.ts`, four CSS modules, `video-data.test.ts`) and `e2e/library-video-others.spec.ts`.

## Video, Films (`/collection/video/films`)

After juliencalot.com: the ten official films (`FILM_IDS`, the owner's order), one to a screen, on an endless vertical strip of its own (wheel, drag, arrow and Page keys; not the page's scroll). Past film 10 comes film 1, and back from film 1 comes film 10. A slide's picture lags behind the slide (`PARALLAX`); its words are moved back by as much as the slide moves, so they stay still and the slide's edge uncovers them (two transforms, no clip). On a wide screen the film covers the screen (`--cover`); on a tall one the whole frame fits, with plain black above and below it, and "Whole frame" switches. The bar at the foot: Index (a contents sheet that jumps to a film), play, previous, next, counter and time, CC where a film has English captions (drawn by the room from a script-built text track), Whole frame, Sound (the site's one sound switch; a press is what lets a browser play sound). The time line is a Web Animation set from the film's clock. Keys: Space or K, arrows, C, F, I. A film that ends slides on to the next. Deep link `?i=<id>&t=<seconds>`.

**Round two (11 Nov 2026).** Outside full screen the time line is one thin line along the very foot of the screen (2 px over a faint track), a Web Animation of `scaleX` set from the film's clock; it thickens on hover or focus. (A first round two version with a handle, end labels and a hover bubble was too obvious on the page, so the owner asked for the subtle one back, and asked to keep the obvious one for full screen.) Press or drag on it to jump (one seek at a time, and one where the press ends), or use the arrow, Page, Home and End keys. Its hit area is 0.9 rem tall and the bar stands 8 px clear above it, so a pointer on its way to a button leaves the line at once. The time ("00:09 / 03:00") stands under the counter on the left of the bar. In full screen a plain row above the bar takes the thin line's place (`timeline.tsx`): the time at its start and the length at its end, the buffered part, the played part and a handle, a time bubble that follows the pointer, and a hit area a little taller while the pointer is on it. Click, drag (mouse or finger) and the same keys jump. The rows are 12 px apart, so the hit area never reaches a button, and the bar's own time is hidden there. The row hides and comes back with the rest of the controls, and stays shown while the pointer is on it or a drag goes on. With the focus on either line, the room's other keys (I, C, F, Space) still work. The controls are one panel that owns its area: a near miss of a button does nothing (before this, the gap between the line and the buttons fell through to the film, so the cursor ring changed and a press paused the film or started a strip drag), a drag from it still moves the strip, and only a press on the film plays or pauses it; the strip takes the pointer only once a press has moved 6 px. On phones the loading percentage stands centred just above the bar. Full screen (`fullscreen.ts`) puts the room itself full screen and asks for landscape (ignored where refused); an iPhone plays the film in its own player with the captions shown there. In full screen the whole frame is fitted in view whatever Whole frame said (the switch and the F key are off there, and the previous mode comes back on leaving), and the words (pair, title, length, kind, counter and time) are neither shown nor drawn; subtitles stay. The controls (the bar, the line, Index) fade out after 2.5 s with nothing done (`idle.ts`, `data-idle` on the room) and fade back with a pointer move, a key, a press, a touch or the wheel. They stay while the pointer is over them or the contents are open, and a press while they are hidden only shows them. Meanwhile the pointer hides over the film and the subtitles settle lower. The contents sheet is drawn inside the room while full screen, and Esc and the browser's own control are followed. Around a film that does not fill the screen there is plain black (no backdrop; the poster shows under the frame until the film has a picture). The words are in a layer above the strip: each line is placed by an outer box, which glides with the frame when Whole frame changes the layout, and moved by an inner one while the strip scrolls (`wordsAt`): the outgoing lines lift and fade, nothing shows at mid scroll, the incoming lines rise in one after another, rippling the way the strip moves, for the wheel, a drag, the keys and the Index jump. At 800 px and below Index sits at the top beside the back button; elsewhere it is first in the bar (the icon only bar starts at 900 px). The contents close on Esc, Close or a press on empty space and give the focus back to Index; on narrow screens the header with "Films" and Close stays at the top, Close has a margin and a touch size, and each film is a large card with the picture across it and the title and description below (two across in landscape and on a tablet). Subtitles are on at every visit (nothing is stored). `&from=<room>` sends the back button to that room (`back.tsx`, `returnOf`). `window.__filmsRoom.peek(pos)` draws the strip at a place for frame sheets. The backdrop pictures are no longer used (the pipeline still makes them; they can go).

**Loading big video.** A film is never loaded whole (35 to 200 MB each). `film-pool.ts` holds one `<video>` per film outside React. The loading list (`preload.ts`) waits for the first film's poster and its first 6 seconds buffered, in the very element the page then shows (the bar is seconds buffered over 6, with bytes estimated from the bitrate). On a page change it waits for the address to change to know which film `?i=&t=` names. A film starts, and goes on after running out of data, only with seconds in hand (6 at start, 4 after a stall), showing the real percentage meanwhile ("042%" in the bottom bar), so a slow line waits and never stutters. The next film buffers once the current one has 8 seconds in hand; only the previous, current and next films keep an element, the rest are released. A stall of 20 s or an error starts the film again (`lib/retry.ts`). A file that is gone (404 or 410), or that this browser cannot play, shows its poster and Try again. A browser that stops fetching while paused (`suspend`, network idle) counts as ready, so a browser that loads only on play never holds the page. The page waits for the first frame with `expectReady`.

**Sound.** The film plays with sound only when the site sound is on and the visitor has pressed something on the site (the shelf click does that); otherwise it plays muted and the Sound button shows a hint.

**Pipeline** `scripts/library/video_films/build.py` (README beside it): each film once, at the source's own size and frame rate (never scaled down). H.264 High, CRF 18, preset slow, AAC 192k, faststart. The three Ruby and Angela films were already H.264 High and are only remuxed (a second encode would lose quality). Two films (the Selena and Obsidia trailer, the Layla and Fanny trailer) have black bars baked into the picture and were cropped to their real picture (1920x808 and 1920x820). Total 1.1 GB. Output keys `video/films/<id>/` in the mirror: `video.mp4`, `poster.webp` (1280, the thumbnail's own size), `thumb.webp`, `en.vtt` where a source has English subtitles. Measured quality on 20 s cuts at CRF 18: VMAF 97.7 (AV1 2560x1080), 99.1 (VP9 1080p). Three films average about 10 Mbit/s, so a 10 Mbit line refills on them: the room waits behind its real percentage instead of stuttering.

Files: `features/library/video/films/` (`page.tsx`, `preload.ts`, `stage.tsx`, `deck.ts`, `motion.ts`, `film-pool.ts`, `film-math.ts` and its test, `captions.ts`, `controls.tsx`, `contents.tsx`, `types.ts`, `films.module.css`), `e2e/library-video-films.spec.ts`. `window.__filmsRoom.snapshot()` is a read-only probe hook.

## Animation (`/collection/animation`, `/collection/animation/<slug>`)

A copy of the hero of apechain.com (`features/library/animation/`). Both routes share `app/collection/animation/layout.tsx` (`AnimationShell`), so the panel in front and its stage stay alive when the address changes. The order is by pair: Selena, Obsidia, Guinevere, Vexana, Lesley, Chang'e, Ruby, Angela, Layla, Fanny.

- **The ring** (`ring.ts`, `ring-driver.ts`, `panel.tsx`). A 35 degree camera and a ring of six slots, squashed to half its depth, tilted 0.18 rad back and turned 0.12 rad, with the front panel on the screen plane. Ten DOM panels share one `preserve-3d` space (no WebGL). Each panel is a box the size of the screen. Its picture is the full screen view, seen through a window of two clips that only translate (`features/story/morph/morph-math.ts`, imported, not edited). One 3D transform puts it on the ring, and that transform has the same list of functions on the ring and flat (`placement`, `FLAT`). The far side of the ring fades out and the backs of the panels are in shade. Every panel and every thumbnail shows the character's splash art (`splashFor`, cover, its own `object-position`). Only the panel in front is live: its stage sits in a box of the recording's own shape (`frameBox`), placed so it covers the screen, and fades in over the splash; as the ring turns away the live stage fades back to the splash (`fadeLive`).
- **Motion** (the reference's numbers). A step takes 1.15 s in power2 in and out (0.65 s for a press). A drag turns the ring 0.75 of a panel per screen width and springs back if it moved less than 5 percent of a panel. The pointer leans the ring up to 2.6 degrees, written at most once a frame. **Nothing turns the ring by itself** (the idle creep and the drift were removed on 11 Nov 2026): it moves only on wheel (one step per gesture, `feedWheel`), drag, swipe, keys, the round buttons and the thumbnails. Turns are Web Animations; a drag writes the transforms. The ring has no ends.
- **Arrival.** Her stage mounts once her welcome and idle loop are in memory (`use-core.ts`, which shows the real bytes on the panel meanwhile), and it plays the welcome every time, then the idle loop for ever. The next character warms first, then the one before; anything further away is released.
- **Launch and the way back.** The button, a press on the panel or Enter holds the transition router, pushes the address and lays the panel flat while its window opens (1.15 s, no cut). Back (the Animation link, Esc) first takes her out of a pose or a waiting press, turns Whole frame off with the morph and leaves full screen, then reverses the launch into the panel's place on the ring. The address leads, so browser history runs the same moves. A failed or stuck move ends in place (a watchdog 6 s after it should have ended) and releases the router. A direct visit fades in once the page is uncovered.
- **Full screen** (`full-ui.tsx`, `full-state.ts`, `use-controls.ts`). Her animation and her buttons, with no time and no player bar. The buttons are a state machine that follows the stage engine (`fullPhase`, `optionViews`, `roleOf`; the room carries the phase as `data-phase`), so only what can be pressed shows: **first-welcome** only Back (no option, no skip); **idle** every option, revealed with a short stagger; **loading** the pressed one alone, "Loading N%" (real bytes) while its clip downloads, then "Starting" (the engine's wait for the frame), a second press cancels; **pose** only "Back to idle"; **action** and **leaving** nothing until she is back in her idle pose; **welcome-again** nothing until the welcome is done, no skip. A button's role comes from the engine's timeline (a page scene is a loop pose, a short scene is an action), never from names. A pose loops until Back to idle. Keys 1 to 9 press only a button that shows. Hover: a fill sweeps under the label, the ring brightens, the pill moves in, the glyph nudges (transform and opacity). Her other clips load in the background and a button shows its clip's loading until it arrives. Clips by kind (the data file agrees with the engine, a test checks it): Selena has the poses Phone, Flight, Lean and the actions Silverbolt, Badge, Right arm; Obsidia Reach, Rest, Perch and Face, Feet, Weapon; every other character has one pose and three actions (Guinevere Face, Feet, Waist; Vexana Face, Left hand, Guardian; Lesley Head, Gun, Foot; Chang'e Body, Moon; Ruby Face, Hand, Scythe; Angela Companion, Crystal, Hand; Layla Face, Feet, Hand; Fanny Blade, Face, Waist).
- **Tall screens** (aspect ratio below 1, `data-shape="tall"`). One **Actions** button at the bottom centre opens a sheet with the real options (closes on Close, Esc or a press outside). The button becomes the pressed option's state or "Back to idle", and hides while nothing can be pressed. Beside Back there are **Full screen** (the Fullscreen API on the room, a landscape lock on a phone, ignored where refused; hidden on iPhone, which has no element full screen) and **Whole frame** (`wholeFrame`: the stage's frame box scaled with one transform to fit the width, plain black bands, `data-whole`). A short landscape screen (`data-low`, 520 px high or less) keeps the options on the right, smaller, with the full screen button. In full screen the site's top bar is out of view, so its sound and song buttons are not reachable there.
- **The field** (`background.tsx`, `slats/`, `colors.ts`). React Bits' MicroSlats (the owner's copy of its source is the reference) ported to TypeScript, one `ogl` canvas. The cursor or a finger pushes a small fluid that bends the swell and leaves light; a press throws a splash. Each character has a sea preset, a slat size, a slat tone, a crest tone and a ground (`slatsLook`), with the pairs sharing a kind of sea. Slat tones are deepened until white reads on them at 4.5 to 1, and the lower rows calm down under the words. A new character rolls in from the horizon over 1.3 s. The field draws nothing while the full screen view covers it or the tab is hidden, caps its canvas at 3.6 MP and a pixel ratio of 2, and measured 2.8 to 3.9 ms of main thread per frame for the whole page at 1440x900 (0.1 to 0.3 ms its own share). `window.__slats` has `snapshot()` and `holdWash(ms)` for frame sheets. The contour lines are gone.
- **Splash art** (`splash.ts`, made by `scripts/library/animation/splash.py`). The picture shown before a welcome starts is the character's official splash, with the game logo strip cropped off the left on eight of them, at 1440 high (2.9 MB for the ten, in `public/img/library/animation/splash/`). `splashFor(slug)` returns the `src` and an `object-position` that centres the face on an upright phone and keeps it in the upper part of a phone on its side.
- **Theme music** (`theme-music.ts`, `theme/`). Selena: Gray New World. Obsidia: Lullaby of Oblivion. Guinevere and Vexana: their own Cross x Credo versions. Lesley and Chang'e: Chang'e & Lesley Music. Ruby and Angela: Angela & Ruby Music. Layla and Fanny: We Believe!. `useThemeMusic(slug)` is called once in the shell, so it plays on the carousel and on a character's page, loops, and goes on across a pair that shares a song; a different song cross fades in once the ring has rested for 450 ms. The song is loaded whole into memory through the site's audio engine and respects the sound switch (off pauses it in place, on resumes it inside that same press). It waits for the visitor's first press, stops the Music room's player when it starts, and fades out and is released when the visitor leaves the room. The first character's song is waited for in the loading list, the neighbours' are deferred. The top bar's record button shows the song title. `window.__themeMusic.snapshot()` is the probe.
- **Loading.** `items()` waits for the data, the chunk, the ten splash pictures (2.9 MB), her stage still, her welcome and idle loop (41 to 43 MB for Selena and Obsidia at 1440x900, 77 to 81 MB at 1920x1080) and her song (`themeItem`); the next character and the one before are deferred, each song listed once (a pair shares a song id, and `runPreload` keeps the last item with an id). `characterItems(slug)` waits for the data, the chunk, her pictures, her welcome and idle loop and her song. Known gap: `routeItems` passes only the pathname, so a jump into the room through the library search (`?i=animation-angela-crystal`) waits for the remembered or first character, and the room loads the named one behind its own panel bar.
- **Hooks.** `[data-animation-room]` carries `data-view`, `data-active`, `data-target`, `data-core`, `data-stage`, `data-phase`, `data-shape`, `data-low`, `data-whole` and `data-fullscreen`; every panel has `data-panel="<slug>"`. To look at frames set `window.__animMorph = { freeze: true }` before a move, wait for `phase === "held"`, then call `seek(ms)` and `release()`.
- Specs: `e2e/library-animation.spec.ts`, `e2e/library-animation-look.spec.ts`. The stage engine under `features/character/` is reused unchanged.

## Music (`/collection/music`)

A field of discs, a copy of a24.raviklaassens.com in the site's look. Seven tracks, in the owner's order (11 Nov 2026): Gray New World, Lullaby of Oblivion, Cross x Credo: Vexana version, Cross x Credo: Guinevere version, Chang'e & Lesley Music, Angela & Ruby Music, We Believe!. Every track is a disc. The field turns without end (past the last disc the first comes back, both ways; the player's queue wraps the same way). The disc in front is the one the words describe, and a press plays it.

- **The scene** (raw three.js, drawn only while something moves: 1.4 s after a scroll, and while a track plays). The disc has a bevelled rim, three ridged hub rings, a real hole and a dark iridescent back. Each face is official art with a ring of text along the rim (title, credits, release) and the title printed on the disc in the site's fonts. The tilt, arc, lean while moving, trailing far discs, glide easing, wheel stepping, drag with throw and spring, pointer nudge and hover tilt follow the reference's behaviour. A side disc glides to the front on a press; the front disc dips and plays through the site's player; dragging the front disc turns it in 3D.
- **The record always rests upright.** The disc turns while its track plays. When the track pauses, stops or changes, it goes on the way it turned and slows down onto a whole turn, so its picture rests upright (`spin.ts`: one curve that starts at the record's speed, 0.8 to 3.6 s, never turning back). A drag of the front disc eases back to the nearest whole turn on each axis. A pooled disc forgets its turns when it leaves its slot. The small disc of the player bar does the same with a Web Animation (`record-spin.ts`). Before this, a disc stopped midway (at about half a turn the picture is upside down), a reused disc kept its old angle, and the bar's disc froze mid-turn. The ring of words along the rim runs clockwise, so along the bottom of a resting disc it reads upside down: by design.
- **The drawn line** is our own: the track's loudness (`wave`, 240 steps) wrapped once round its disc like a needle trace. It draws itself on hover, on keyboard focus and for 1.4 s after a scroll. While a track plays it is faint and the played part is drawn in `#7c4dff` with a moving head. Drawn in WebGL, only shader settings change, never the shape.
- **Words.** The title block top left (title, credits, year), then the platforms of a released single: Spotify, Apple Music and YouTube as their marks (from Simple Icons) and "All platforms", each in a new tab (`links` in the data; only Gray New World and Lullaby of Oblivion have them). Under the disc: previous, Play, Download, Watch, then next. At 991 px and below Watch sits at the top beside the Collection button and opens its list downward. No ratings, no switcher, `BackToCollection`, a transparent bar, a takedown line under the play row.
- **Player.** One slim bar above the front disc on wide screens and at the foot on narrow ones. Where the download was there is a loop button: it repeats the track it was set on for ever (`aria-pressed`), and the next track the visitor chooses plays once. The download under the disc stays.
- **Watch and the way back.** Each film link is `videoHref(id, t, "music")`, so the Video rooms' close and back come back to Music (`returnOf` in `video/links.ts`). The room keeps the track in front in `sessionStorage` (`aspirants:music-place`) and opens on it whenever the address has no `?i=`. "Soundtrack video" opens the soundtrack film at the owner's time for that track.
- **Layout.** At 991 px wide and below the field is a row with the neighbours peeking in. On wider screens it sits right of and below the middle (`shiftX`, `shiftY`) and shrinks a little on short screens (`fullHeight`, `minFit`).
- **Faces, audio and wave** come only from `scripts/library/music/discs.py` and `build.py`. Never edit them by hand. Seven faces, 805 KB, at `music/discs/*.webp` (R2, with CORS), not in `public/`. A key in the mirror is never written again: a changed file gets a new key (Chang'e & Lesley moved to `music/change-lesley-display-v2.m4a` when Angela & Ruby Music, 644.50 to 722.80 s of the soundtrack film, was cut out of its end).
- **Loading.** The loading list holds the data, the room chunk and the faces as one item that counts their bytes together. The scene calls `expectReady("library-music-scene")`. The scene is imported statically in `music/page.tsx` (a nested lazy import made dev print a preload link to a chunk that did not exist).
- **Failures.** Without WebGL, or if the scene fails, the page is the plain list (`track-list.tsx`). It is always in the HTML and inert while the scene runs. One `h1`.
- **Keys.** Arrows turn the field, Home and End jump to the ends, Enter plays the disc in front, Space plays and pauses, N and P change track, Q opens the queue, plus and minus change the volume.
- **Address.** `?i=<id>` opens the room on a disc. Without it, the remembered track. The address follows the disc in front once the visitor turns the field.
- **Hooks.** `window.__musicRoom.snapshot()` gives each disc on screen, its turns and whether it is upright.
- Removed on 11 Nov 2026: Obsidia: Aspirant, Obsidia: Solo and the soundtrack in full (their files stay in the bucket, unused). Removed earlier with the redesign: search, the kind filter and "Play next".
- Files: `features/library/music/` (`engine.ts`, `field.ts`, `wheel.ts`, `spin.ts`, `record-spin.ts`, `place.ts`, `trace.ts`, `trace-mesh.ts`, `disc-shape.ts`, `disc-materials.ts`, `disc-art.ts`, `faces.ts`, `disc-scene.tsx`, `platform-links.tsx`, `reveal.ts`, `pop-menu.tsx`, `track-list.tsx`, `curtain.ts`, `player-store.ts`, `player-bar.tsx`, `preload.ts`, `logic.ts`, `types.ts`, `music.module.css`, tests) and `e2e/library-music.spec.ts`.
